Australia · Digital consumer behaviour

From Clicks to Cart

Online shopping has become an important part of how Australians purchase goods and interact with businesses. But what does the data reveal about the growth of online retail, the activities people undertake online, and the differences between consumer groups?

This visual investigation brings together Australian retail turnover statistics, online activity survey results and capital-city demographic data to explore the changing relationship between Australians and digital commerce.

Explore the charts in sequence, moving from the retail market as a whole to individual shopping behaviours and demographic context.

01

The market

The rise of online retail

Before exploring individual shoppers, it is useful to understand the broader retail environment. Retail turnover provides context for the scale and development of online sales in Australia. The following charts examine overall trends, differences between food and non-food sales, and the influence of seasonal patterns.

Looking at the market as a whole establishes the context. The next question is how different retail categories contribute to this activity and whether their trajectories move together.

02

Retail categories

What Australians buy online

Online retail is not one uniform market. Different categories can follow different patterns as consumer needs, shopping habits and retail conditions change. These comparisons help reveal where category trends diverge and how monthly movements affect the overall picture.

Retail statistics show what is happening in the market. Survey data offers another perspective by examining what people report doing online and how participation differs across groups.

03

Consumer behaviour

Who shops online, and what do they do?

Shopping participation is only one part of digital consumer behaviour. People also use online services for communication, information, entertainment and other everyday activities. Comparing survey results can help place online shopping within this wider digital landscape.

Overall participation can conceal differences between demographic groups. Looking more closely at age, gender and location can help identify where online behaviour varies, while remembering that observed differences do not automatically explain their causes.

04

Demographic differences

Understanding the people behind the data

Consumer behaviour may differ across age groups, genders and places. These charts examine the demographic patterns available in the supplied data. Demographic measures provide context, but they should not be treated as direct evidence of shopping preferences unless the dataset explicitly measures those preferences.

Geographic differences in online activity can also reflect the distinction between metropolitan and regional communities. The next comparison considers location directly, where the survey data permits it.

05

Geography and change

Where people live, and how behaviour changes

Access, infrastructure and local circumstances may shape how people engage with digital services. Comparing metropolitan and regional responses can highlight differences in reported activity. Comparing survey years can also show whether measured behaviours have changed, provided the survey questions and populations are sufficiently comparable.

Together, the market statistics and survey results provide complementary views: one describes retail activity, while the other describes reported online behaviour. Their findings should be interpreted according to what each dataset actually measures.

Conclusion

From clicks to cart: bringing the evidence together

Understanding online shopping in Australia requires more than one measure. Retail turnover can reveal changes in market activity, category comparisons can show differences within retail, and survey data can describe how people report using online services.

The most useful conclusions emerge when these perspectives are considered together, while accounting for differences in time periods, definitions and populations. Rather than assuming that one trend explains another, the evidence can be used to identify questions for further investigation.

As you explore the charts, consider three questions: Which patterns remain consistent over time? Which groups or categories differ? What additional data would help explain the reasons behind those differences?

About the data

This project combines ABS retail turnover data, ACMA survey measures and capital-city demographic information. These sources describe different aspects of retail activity and digital behaviour, so their measures should not be treated as directly interchangeable.

Capital-city median age and sex ratio are demographic context; they do not measure online shopping behaviour directly. Any comparisons across survey years should account for differences in question wording, sampling and definitions where relevant.
